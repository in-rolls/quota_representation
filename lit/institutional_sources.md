# Institutional background: source checks

Checked 7 November 2026 for the four-paragraph revision of Section 3.

- **Office and responsibilities:** Rajasthan Panchayati Raj Act (1994), sections 12, 32, and 50; UP Panchayat Raj Act (1947, amended text), sections 11-B, 11-F, 12, and 15. Cite formal council/head responsibilities, not demonstrated personal authority. The GP head is a sarpanch in Rajasthan and a pradhan in UP. Rajasthan's block-level head is also called a pradhan; do not conflate the offices.
- **Quota minima:** Constitution Article 243D(4) concerns head offices; Article 243D(3) concerns council seats. Ministry of Panchayati Raj status report dated 8 September 2021, p. 2, records Rajasthan's 50% amendment notified 11 April 2008; p. 4 explicitly lists UP among states without a 50% provision and states a minimum of one third. The Ministry's 2024 upload date is not the report date. Do not use the older Rajasthan Act compilation's one-third wording to describe the post-2008 minimum.
- **Eligibility:** Rajasthan Act section 16 and UP Act section 11-A. Women may contest non-quota offices; caste restrictions can still apply. 'Open' in this section refers to gender eligibility, not removal of every caste restriction.
- **Rajasthan assignment:** Election Rules (1994), Rule 9(3), specifies a draw of lots for women-reserved head offices. Official India Code indexed text and the reproduced rule were checked; the PDF endpoint is intermittently inaccessible. This is a statement of the rule, not a reconstruction of all historical eligible pools. Rule 7's ward/constituency exclusions cannot automatically be transferred to head offices.
- **UP assignment:** Bose and Das (2018), p. 206, describes serial lists in a study of nine districts in 2005 and 2010. Chaturvedi, Das, and Mahajan (2025), p. 760, describes a fresh 2015 allocation after delimitation using caste-specific population rankings within blocks. Local published PDFs and page-specific records are in `papers/` and `studies/`. The two accounts have different temporal scope.
- **Terms:** Constitution Article 243E specifies five years, subject to earlier dissolution. The text says 'normally' because early dissolution and delayed elections should not be ruled out.
- **Observed histories:** Adjacent-election and four-election panels contain repeated reservations and reserved terms followed by open terms. These descriptive histories do not establish a unique legal rotation algorithm. The linked samples in `../quota_assignment` differ from this paper's panels; do not substitute their sample counts or interpret transition dependence alone as proof against randomization.

Official URLs and publication metadata are in `ms/quota_representation.bib`. The institutional revision changes exposition and citations, not analysis data, specifications, or estimates.
