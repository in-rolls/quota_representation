# Winners' subsequent candidacy

This exploratory analysis was specified after reviewing the manuscript, schemas, sample coverage, and caste transitions. No estimates of the quota contrast had been examined. A design reviewer examined linkage counts during planning; this is not a preregistration.

- Question: Among linked GPs whose next head election is unrestricted by gender or caste, how often can the prior winner be identified in the next candidate roster, and how does this differ between prior quota and non-quota seats?
- Cohorts: Rajasthan winners in 2015 followed to 2020; UP winners in 2015 followed to 2021. Include all prior non-quota winners, irrespective of sex. Unit: one prior winner/GP. Equal weight per GP for raw rates.
- Scope: subsequent candidacy for the same office in the linked GP. Candidacy elsewhere or for another office is not observed by this comparison. This is distinct from women's overall candidacy, later female wins, or the effect of winning office on a fixed person's career.
- Eligibility: next election has no gender or caste restriction. Do not condition on prior winner caste to define eligibility. Conditioning on subsequent reservation, geography linkage and available records limits interpretation; report conditional cohort contrasts rather than an unconditional quota ATE.
- Sources: existing hash-pinned Rajasthan 2015–2020 panel and 2020 candidate/winner events; UP 2015–2021 panel and 2021 candidate records, resolved through `00_config.R`.
- Coverage: one-to-one GP panels. Rajasthan requires unique election events, unique candidate identifiers, exactly one winner event, and candidate count equal to the reported number contesting. UP requires unique candidate IDs, nonempty names, and one declared winner whose name agrees with the panel. UP lacks an independent count of all contestants; this cannot establish roster completeness.
- Linkage: Unicode normalization, lower case, punctuation/format removal and whitespace normalization; preserve letters and diacritics. Rajasthan requires a unique candidate-name match with no known sex conflict. UP primary requires a unique candidate-name and father/husband-name match with no known sex conflict. UP name-only sensitivity uses the same baseline-name/relative-complete sample. Compact-name sensitivity removes spaces in addition to the common normalization. No probabilistic links are accepted as identities.
- Uncertainty: multiple matching candidates and known sex-conflicting matches are unresolved, not returns or nonreturns. Use the same denominator across linkage rules within state, excluding these cases under any rule. Exact nonmatches count as zero only for the measured *detected-match* outcome; they do not prove nonparticipation. Neither matching rule is a formal bound on actual recontesting.
- Estimation: raw detected-match rates by prior quota status; linear probability model with baseline block fixed effects and baseline caste-reservation indicators, clustering at baseline block with the manuscript's SSC convention and cluster-t 95% intervals. The adjusted comparison includes all baseline winners, not only women in non-quota seats. Report baseline rate, counts, intervals, clusters and exclusions.
- Sensitivity: name-only and whitespace-insensitive linkage on a common within-state sample. Inspect exact links, ambiguous links, sex disagreements and near-name nonmatches. Do not tune matching to the estimated contrast.
- Interpretation: continued participation is one route from quota officeholding to future women's representation. Low detected return rates alone do not imply no entry by other women, and linkage failure may vary by prior quota status. Qualification/authority mechanisms are not identified by this exercise.
- Verification: independently reconstruct eligibility and matching from the pinned sources; check key uniqueness, row conservation, candidate-count coverage, sex conflicts and numerator/denominator arithmetic. Independently rederive the fixed-effects estimates and clustered variances. Generate tables, figure and prose macros from the same results.

## Extension following the author's request

After seeing the exact/compact estimates, the author requested transliteration, edit-distance sensitivity and age checks, bounded to an appendix. No earlier same-person recontesting analysis was located in the working tree or targeted Git-history search; existing Appendix H concerns aggregate women's candidacy. UP's existing English winner-name field is repeated across each GP's candidate rows and is not a transliteration of each candidate's own name, so it must not be used for candidate identity. Use deterministic ICU transliteration on both rounds' original names instead.

Add an explicitly generous scenario allowing a same-GP candidate whose Latin-transliterated, space-free name has Levenshtein distance at most two and at most 20% of the longer name. Nonexact matches require both names to have at least five characters. UP additionally reports a supported fuzzy rule requiring relative names to agree within three edits and 25%; the generous scenario does not require relative agreement. Both scenarios count any compatible candidate, including multiple possibilities, as a possible return; they are sensitivity scenarios, not resolved identities or formal upper bounds.

No baseline age is recorded in Rajasthan 2015, and the upstream UP 2015 declared-winner age fields are entirely missing. Follow-up age can nevertheless exclude implausible identities: ages below 25 in Rajasthan 2020 or 26 in UP 2021 are inconsistent with having reached 21 in 2015, allowing for within-year election dates. Retain missing ages as unknown; known age conflicts in exact matches join the unresolved exclusions used for every rule. Require compatible recorded sex for fuzzy matches. The constitutional age reference is Article 243F (official text: https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/pdf1/Part9.pdf). These checks cannot establish aging consistency without baseline age.
